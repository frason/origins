## Priority
High

## Goal
Replace effectively constant producer abundance with a deterministic biomass ecology where dense animal populations can exhaust local resources, damage habitat, and trigger meaningful starvation, reduced reproduction, migration, and adaptation.

## Problem
The current system removes biomass when herbivores feed, but every cell receives additive producer growth each tick from a persistent solar-energy value. Growth does not consume or require nutrients or moisture, corpse nutrients can permanently increase cell energy, producer biomass has no maintenance loss, and grazers can remove more biomass than their remaining energy capacity can use. Global biomass across 10,000 cells also hides severe local depletion. This lets dense colonies remain indefinitely concentrated in productive regions.

## Scope
- Separate renewable energy flux from stored/consumable ecological resources so solar input does not behave like an endlessly reusable stock.
- Replace additive producer growth with bounded logistic growth based on current biomass and carrying capacity.
- Make growth respond to locally available nutrients and, after #41 decisions, moisture/soil conditions.
- Limit grazing consumption by animal energy headroom and producer edibility/defense.
- Add deterministic producer maintenance or mortality so biomass does not remain permanently at carrying capacity.
- Model overgrazing pressure and gradual habitat recovery without hardcoded map-side or species favoritism.
- Preserve biome-specific producer archetypes and God Mode controls.
- Add local/regional observability: occupied-tile biomass, depleted-area share, grazing pressure, and recovery trend; keep total world biomass but do not present it alone.

## Done when
- [ ] A dense herbivore colony measurably reduces local biomass faster than it regrows.
- [ ] Sustained overgrazing lowers local food availability and affects animal energy, reproduction, or survival.
- [ ] Abandoned depleted cells recover gradually rather than instantly or never.
- [ ] An animal never consumes more producer biomass than it can convert into available energy capacity.
- [ ] Nutrients influence producer growth and are consumed or otherwise bounded; corpse recycling cannot permanently ratchet solar productivity upward.
- [ ] Desert, tundra, grassland, forest, and wetland retain meaningfully different productivity and recovery behavior.
- [ ] UI metrics distinguish global abundance from scarcity where animals actually live.
- [ ] Same seed and settings produce identical biomass and creature state.
- [ ] Tests cover depletion, recovery, carrying capacity, appetite-limited grazing, nutrient bounds, biome differences, and deterministic replay.
- [ ] Long-run sustainability and performance tests pass without weakening existing ecosystem quality gates.

## Implementation notes
Treat this as an ecological systems change, not a visual redistribution shortcut. Calibrate against several fixed seeds and compare both local habitat occupancy and whole-world totals. Consider splitting implementation into producer growth/consumption, soil-moisture integration, and UI metrics if the issue becomes too large.
